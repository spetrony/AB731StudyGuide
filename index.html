Domain 1 · Identify the Business Value of Generative AI Solutions

35–40% of the exam · the heaviest domain. Focuses on foundational concepts, choosing the right AI solution, cost drivers, challenges, and the mechanics of generative AI.

1.1 Foundational concepts — AI vs. generative AI

AI is a toolbox of capabilities that help computers perform tasks that once required human judgment — language understanding, image recognition, pattern spotting, recommendations.

Generative AI is a subset of AI that creates content — text, code, images, video. It powers new productivity and creativity scenarios.

The four capability types

TypeWhat it doesExamples
DescriptiveDescribes what happenedSearch, classification, summarization
PredictiveAnticipates what will happenForecasting, anomaly detection, scoring
PrescriptiveRecommends what to doRecommendations, optimization
GenerativeCreates new contentDrafts, images, code, summaries

Data science, ML, deep learning

  • Data science turns data into actionable insight using statistics, engineering, and domain expertise.
  • Machine learning learns patterns from data to make predictions or classifications.
  • Deep learning uses layered neural networks to find complex patterns in unstructured data (images, text, audio). Needs lots of data and compute.
For business leaders: the point isn't the algorithm — it's where capabilities deliver outcomes: faster decisions, better customer experience, lower cost to serve.
1.2 Selecting the right AI solution for a business need

Start with the business problem, not the technology. Match the capability to the need.

Business needCapability to choose
Search and summarize documentsDescriptive / generative
Forecast revenue, predict churnPredictive (machine learning)
Recommend products or next best actionPrescriptive
Draft policies, marketing copy, emailsGenerative AI
Detect fraud, flag anomaliesPredictive ML

Where generative AI provides business value

  • Scalability — handle huge volumes without proportional headcount.
  • Automation — eliminate repetitive drafting, summarization, and research work.
  • Productivity — drafts, summaries, options produced faster.
  • Speed and scale — AI analyzes large volumes and synthesizes insights in minutes.
  • Innovation — intelligent search, document understanding, agentic workflows.
1.3 AI models — pretrained vs. fine-tuned
TypeDefinitionWhen to use
Pretrained modelTrained on a general, broad dataset; ready to use out of the box.General tasks — drafting, summarizing, Q&A on common topics.
Fine-tuned modelPretrained model further trained on your domain-specific data.Specialized vocabulary, unique tone, or task requiring high accuracy on niche topics.
Foundation modelLarge pretrained model that can be adapted to many downstream tasks (base for generative AI).Creative or reasoning-heavy tasks, broad generation.
Small Language Model (SLM)Smaller, faster, cheaper model.Latency-sensitive or cost-sensitive use cases; on-device or edge.
Prebuilt servicesTurnkey task-specific APIs (Vision, Document Intelligence, Language, Search).Well-defined, common tasks.
Right-size principle: not every task needs the largest model. Larger models cost more and are slower — use the smallest capable option.
1.4 Cost drivers — tokens, ROI, and usage

Tokens

A token is a chunk of text the model processes (roughly ¾ of a word in English). Both input and output tokens cost money. Generative AI usage is typically billed per 1,000 tokens.

Longer prompts, longer context, and longer responses = more tokens = higher cost.

Main cost levers

  • Model size — bigger models cost more per token.
  • Prompt size — long grounding context inflates input tokens.
  • Response length — unbounded outputs are expensive.
  • Usage volume — number of calls per day / user.
  • Fine-tuning and custom deployments — add training and hosting costs.

ROI considerations

Measure ROI across productivity (hours saved), quality (error reduction, CSAT), and revenue (faster cycle times, new capabilities). Include the total cost of ownership: licensing, compute, data preparation, governance, training, and change management.

Watch out: AI workloads scale dynamically — a pilot with a few thousand transactions can grow to millions. Without pricing visibility, budgets spiral.
1.5 Challenges — fabrication, reliability, bias
ChallengeWhat it meansMitigation
Fabrication (hallucination)Model produces convincing but incorrect information.Grounding, RAG, human review, citations.
ReliabilityOutput varies; model fails in edge cases.Test with representative data, fail safely, set guardrails.
BiasUnequal treatment of similar cases; reflects dataset bias.Diverse datasets, fairness testing, diverse design teams, the Responsible AI Dashboard.
Data sensitivityLeaking PII or confidential info into model inputs/outputs.Data classification, permissions, DLP, grounding on authorized data only.
Over-relianceUsers trust outputs without verification.Prompt literacy, disclosures, human-in-the-loop for consequential decisions.
1.6 Prompt engineering

A prompt is the input that guides a generative AI model. Prompt quality directly controls output quality.

Why prompt engineering matters

  • Improves efficiency (fewer revisions).
  • Aligns outputs with business goals.
  • Reduces risk of misinterpretation.

Core prompt techniques

  • Be specific — state the task, audience, format, and constraints.
  • Provide context — include relevant background the model needs.
  • Give examples (few-shot) — show desired output format.
  • Set the persona / role — "You are a compliance analyst…"
  • Chain of thought — ask the model to reason step-by-step for complex tasks.
  • Iterate — refine based on outputs.
Two audiences: business users need prompt literacy; developers use advanced prompt engineering to optimize solutions.
1.7 Grounding and RAG

Grounding connects AI responses to authoritative data sources so outputs are based on verified information instead of model memory alone.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is the pattern: retrieve relevant documents from a knowledge store, then generate an answer using that retrieved context.

Why it matters

  • Accuracy — reduces convincing-but-wrong answers.
  • Compliance — outputs align with regulations and current policy.
  • Trust — builds confidence with employees and customers.
  • Freshness — incorporates real-time or enterprise-specific info that wasn't in the model's training data.

Grounded vs. ungrounded

  • Ungrounded: "The latest compliance regulation is ISO 27001:2013." — may be outdated.
  • Grounded: "According to your company's compliance portal, the latest is ISO 27001:2022." — verified from internal source.

Microsoft 365 Copilot is a real-world example: it grounds responses in your files, emails, chats, and calendars via Microsoft Graph.

1.8 Data quality, representativeness, and types

AI is only as good as the data behind it.

Data dimensionWhy it matters
Type (structured, unstructured, semi-structured)Different types need different tools — tables vs. documents vs. images.
Quality (accuracy, completeness, freshness)Garbage in, garbage out. Poor quality cascades into unreliable outputs.
RepresentativenessTraining data must reflect the population the model will serve — avoids bias and exclusion.
LabelingSupervised ML needs accurate labels.
PermissionsOnly authorized data should enter prompts — respects least-privilege.
1.9 Machine learning — when it adds value and the lifecycle

When ML adds value

  • Predictive analytics — forecasting sales, demand, risk.
  • Personalization — customer segmentation, recommendations.
  • Automation — anomaly detection, fraud detection.

Gen-AI vs. ML use cases

Generative AI use casesMachine learning use cases
Drafting reportsPredicting customer churn
Creating marketing contentPersonalizing product suggestions
Summarizing documentsDetecting fraud

ML lifecycle (high level)

  1. Define the business problem and success metric.
  2. Collect & prepare data.
  3. Train the model.
  4. Evaluate performance against the metric.
  5. Deploy and integrate (inferencing).
  6. Monitor and retrain as data drifts.

Business leaders own goal-setting, resourcing, and evaluation; data scientists and engineers own the build.

1.10 Secure AI — security considerations for AI systems

Secure AI protects data, models, and applications across the AI lifecycle.

Three security layers

LayerFocus
Application securitySecure code, secure APIs, input validation, prompt injection defenses, rate limiting.
Data securityClassification, encryption at rest and in transit, DLP, least privilege, data residency.
Authentication & identityStrong identity (Entra ID), MFA, RBAC, conditional access, service-to-service auth.

Key threats to know

  • Prompt injection — malicious instructions hidden in user input or retrieved content.
  • Data exfiltration — sensitive content appearing in outputs.
  • Model abuse / jailbreak — users bypassing safety guardrails.
  • Supply-chain risk — compromised third-party models or plugins.

Domain 2 · Microsoft's AI Apps & Services

35–40% of the exam. Maps business needs to Microsoft products: Microsoft 365 Copilot, Copilot Studio, Microsoft Graph, Researcher/Analyst, Microsoft Foundry, and Foundry Tools.

2.1 What Microsoft Copilot is — and isn't

Microsoft Copilot is the umbrella name for AI-powered assistance across Microsoft products — Windows, Edge, GitHub, Microsoft 365. Each experience combines LLMs with relevant context.

Microsoft 365 Copilot is the experience built into Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and Teams. It grounds responses in your organizational data via Microsoft Graph — files, emails, chats, calendars — respecting your existing permissions and compliance.

Three core benefits: boost productivity, improve decision quality, strengthen governance with enterprise-grade security.
2.2 Microsoft 365 Copilot vs. Copilot Chat vs. Copilot Studio
ProductWhat it doesBest for
Microsoft 365 CopilotEmbedded AI in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Teams. Grounded in org data via Microsoft Graph.Knowledge-worker productivity in-app.
Microsoft 365 Copilot ChatConversational web & mobile experience delivering context-aware answers grounded in org data.Quick research, mobile work, cross-app answers.
Microsoft Copilot StudioLow-code platform to build custom copilots and extend existing ones. Connects to external data and systems.Tailoring AI to unique workflows without heavy development.

Copilot in each Microsoft 365 app

  • Word — draft, rewrite for clarity, adjust tone.
  • Excel — analyze trends, create visualizations, model scenarios.
  • PowerPoint — turn ideas and docs into polished decks.
  • Outlook — summarize threads, draft replies, triage inbox.
  • Teams — summarize meetings, surface action items, catch up on chats.
2.3 Microsoft Graph — the backbone of enterprise context

Microsoft Graph is the connective layer linking people, documents, conversations, and calendars. It's how Copilot knows which project a document belongs to, who owns it, and what decisions were made in related meetings.

  • Context-rich answers — Copilot draws from related files, meetings, and chats — not random content.
  • Security & compliance built-in — Graph respects identity and permissions.
  • Breaks down silos — unified, permission-aware view across Microsoft 365.
2.4 Researcher and Analyst in Copilot

Researcher and Analyst are specialized agents inside Microsoft 365 Copilot for deeper, multi-step work.

AgentWhen to useExample scenario
ResearcherComplex, multi-step research that combines web knowledge with your organizational data. Produces thorough, cited analyses.Building a competitive brief pulling from public sources plus internal notes.
AnalystAdvanced data analysis on your content — running code, building charts, reasoning over spreadsheets and documents.Turning a raw CSV into a forecast, variance analysis, or cohort breakdown.
Rule of thumb: Researcher for what and why (insights from many sources). Analyst for how much and what if (quantitative analysis).
2.5 Build, Buy, or Extend — the decision framework
OptionWhen to chooseExample
Buy (use Microsoft 365 Copilot out of the box)Common productivity needs already covered by Copilot.Rolling out Copilot to knowledge workers.
Extend (Copilot Studio, plugins, connectors, agents)Copilot is close but you need integrations with line-of-business systems or specialized workflows.Adding a connector to an ERP so Copilot can answer "what's my inventory of SKU X?".
Build (Microsoft Foundry)You need a custom AI application, fine-tuning, a specialized model, or differentiated experience.A customer-facing support agent with industry-specific tone and domain knowledge.

The Microsoft 365 Copilot extensibility framework covers plugins, connectors, agents, and declarative extensions that plug into the existing Copilot experience.

2.6 Microsoft Foundry — the unified platform

Microsoft Foundry is Azure's unified platform-as-a-service for enterprise AI operations, model building, and app development. It consolidates prior Azure AI Studio / Azure AI Foundry into one platform.

What Foundry unifies

  • Agents, models, and tools under a single management grouping.
  • Enterprise-readiness: tracing, monitoring, evaluations, customizable enterprise setup.
  • Unified RBAC, networking, and policies under one Azure resource provider.

Who Foundry is for

  • App developers building AI-powered products.
  • ML engineers and data scientists fine-tuning models and running evals.
  • IT admins and platform engineers governing AI resources and enforcing policy.

Benefits

  • Scalability — production-grade infrastructure; scales from prototype to enterprise.
  • Security & governance — built-in observability, audit, and RBAC.
  • Flexibility — thousands of models, prebuilt tools, and catalogs.
2.7 Foundry Tools — Azure AI services (Vision, Language, Search, etc.)

Foundry Tools are prebuilt Azure AI services for common, well-defined tasks. Pick these when you want fast time-to-value with built-in safety and predictable costs.

ServiceUse case
Azure Vision in Foundry ToolsImage analysis, OCR, object detection, face analysis.
Azure AI LanguageEntity recognition, sentiment, summarization, translation-adjacent tasks.
Azure AI Document IntelligenceExtract structured data from forms and documents.
Azure AI SearchEnterprise search and the retrieval backbone for RAG applications.
Azure AI SpeechTranscription, TTS, speech translation.
Azure AI Content SafetyDetect harmful content in text and images — safety layer for gen-AI apps.
Rule of thumb: prebuilt Foundry Tools for well-defined tasks · foundation models for generative/reasoning · fine-tuning for specialized accuracy.

2.9 Integrated Microsoft AI solution — risk mitigation & safety benefits

Choosing integrated Microsoft AI (vs. stitching together disparate services) brings:

  • Unified identity & permissions via Entra ID and Microsoft Graph.
  • Built-in Responsible AI controls — content safety, groundedness checks, evaluations.
  • Compliance coverage — Microsoft 365 Copilot inherits your tenant compliance (EU data boundary, DLP, sensitivity labels).
  • Operational tooling — tracing, monitoring, and RBAC built in.
  • Lower total risk — fewer moving parts and a single accountable vendor.

Domain 3 · Implementation & Adoption Strategy

20–25% of the exam. Responsible AI, governance, AI councils, adoption teams, AI champions, and licensing models.

3.1 The six Responsible AI principles at Microsoft

Microsoft's Responsible AI Standard rests on six principles — memorize them.

PrincipleWhat it means
FairnessTreat similar people and cases similarly; test to reduce bias.
Reliability & safetyPerform consistently and fail safely, even in unexpected conditions.
Privacy & securityProtect data throughout its lifecycle; least privilege and purpose limitation.
InclusivenessDesign for diverse needs; test with a broad user base to avoid exclusion.
TransparencyMake the system intelligible — explain how it works and where it may fall short.
AccountabilityAssign clear ownership; review decisions; provide remediation paths.
Mnemonic: FRPITA — Fairness, Reliability, Privacy, Inclusiveness, Transparency, Accountability.

Some Microsoft materials also list safety as its own principle (pairing with reliability). The exam may reference all eight qualities — fairness, reliability, safety, privacy, security, inclusiveness, transparency, and accountability.

3.2 Governance — building the system

Principles only work when you operationalize them. Build a governance system that translates values into repeatable decisions and accountability across the AI lifecycle.

Three governance approaches

ApproachStrengthTrade-off
Chief Ethics Officer (centralized)Fast decisions, clear accountability, visible leadership.Hard to enforce broadly without a team.
Ethics Office (decentralized team)Scale — embed across business units with dedicated staff.Requires funding and sustained attention.
Ethics Committee (cross-functional)Diverse perspectives, external expertise.Slower; advisory rather than operational.
Best practice: a hybrid model — central policy with local operational teams — reporting to the board, with dedicated funding, staffing, and authority.

AI Council

An AI Council provides strategy, oversight, and cross-functional alignment. It typically includes legal, security, compliance, IT, HR, data, and business-unit leaders.

3.3 Sensitive-use review triggers

Microsoft flags a use case as "sensitive" — and subject to extra review — when it falls into any of these categories:

  • Denial of consequential services — AI affects access to financial, housing, insurance, education, employment, or healthcare services.
  • Risk of harm — physical, emotional, or psychological harm to individuals (life-or-death, safety-critical, vulnerable populations, children).
  • Infringement on human rights — freedom, expression, privacy, assembly (e.g., law enforcement, policing).

Review process has three stages: identification → assessment → mitigation. A Responsible AI Champ liaises between employees and central governance.

3.4 Organizing for AI success

Four-step journey to unlock AI value

  1. Educate and inspire — executive envisioning sessions; align on 2–3 strategic bets.
  2. Assess AI readiness — Secure AI Readiness Assessment across five dimensions (business strategy, technology strategy, AI experience, org & culture, AI governance & security). Stages: Exploring → Planning → Implementing → Scaling → Realizing.
  3. Map your AI journey — establish a Center of Excellence, governance & security guardrails, data & platform landing zones.
  4. Start building the agentic future — move from pilots to production with repeatable patterns.

Adoption team

AI transformation is a team sport. Include:

  • Executive sponsor (owns strategy and budget).
  • AI Governance Lead / Chief AI Officer.
  • IT / Security / Compliance.
  • Business unit leaders who own use cases.
  • Data scientists and developers.
  • Change management and communications.
3.5 AI Champions program

AI Champions are employees across departments who advocate for AI adoption, share best practices, and support peers. They:

  • Identify high-value use cases in their teams.
  • Share prompt patterns and success stories.
  • Escalate issues to the central adoption team.
  • Model responsible use.

A champions program turns a top-down rollout into grassroots momentum — critical for scale.

3.6 Common barriers to adoption
  • Unclear business value — no defined use case or ROI hypothesis.
  • Data readiness gaps — poor quality, fragmented, or inaccessible data.
  • Skills gap — teams don't know how to prompt or evaluate outputs.
  • Trust & change resistance — fear of job loss, skepticism of outputs.
  • Security & compliance concerns — no clear governance framework.
  • Cost visibility — no FinOps model for AI workloads.
  • Shadow AI — employees using unsanctioned tools because IT is too slow.

Impacts to plan for

Evaluate impacts on data (volume, classification, residency), security (new threats, access controls), privacy (PII handling, consent), and cost (tokens, storage, licensing, training).

3.7 Copilot licensing — three options
OptionBest forKey benefit
Included with Microsoft 365 subscriptionOrgs already on eligible M365 plans.Simplest; no additional setup.
Monthly subscriptionLong-term adoption and steady usage.Predictable cost; scales over time.
Pay-as-you-goPilots, short-term projects, seasonal workloads.Flexible; no long-term commitment.

Underlying Microsoft 365 license tiers

  • Microsoft 365 E3 — core productivity, basic security.
  • Microsoft 365 E5 — adds advanced threat protection, analytics, premium compliance.
  • Microsoft 365 Business Standard — SMB core collaboration.
  • Microsoft 365 Business Premium — SMB with advanced security and device management.

Copilot availability depends on an eligible underlying Microsoft 365 license. Copilot Chat is included with Microsoft 365 Copilot on web and mobile.

3.8 Azure AI / Foundry subscription models
ModelBest forTrade-off
Pay-as-you-goPilots, exploration, variable workloads.Costs can spike as usage grows.
Pre-paid (committed spend)Mature deployments with stable demand.Requires accurate forecasting; less flexible.

Pair model choice with your adoption stage: start pay-as-you-go when exploring; shift to pre-paid once demand is steady for predictability and discounts.

3.9 Measuring adoption success
  • Productivity metrics — hours saved per user, time-to-task reduction.
  • Quality metrics — error rates, CSAT, NPS.
  • Business metrics — revenue influence, cost-to-serve, cycle time.
  • Usage metrics — active users, feature adoption, prompt volume.
  • Responsible-AI metrics — incidents raised, sensitive-use reviews completed, evaluation scores.
Key leadership questions (from Microsoft's adoption guidance): Do we have a unified AI strategy, not just isolated projects? Are we tracking ROI and outcomes, not just spending? Are our CoEs collaborative or siloed? Are we enabling innovation at every level?
